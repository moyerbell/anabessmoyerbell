2nd Act

Executive Director, 2021 to [confirm end date; she was the outgoing director in May 2024]

Ana Bess brought two nonprofits, Improbable Players and COAAST, together as one, then rebuilt its operations, brand and programming around evidence, equity and a trauma-informed, recovery-friendly workplace. Grant funding and the donor base grew, public health campaigns created new revenue, and the organization won state funding.

She founded COAAST in 2016. It used drama therapy and applied theater to prevent overdose with students, health professionals and people in recovery, in partnership with the Rhode Island Department of Health and NYU’s Theatre and Health Lab.

Office of the Governor, Rhode Island

Senior Advisor on substance use treatment, recovery and harm reduction, 2021 to [confirm]

Ana Bess helped pass the nation’s first Overdose Prevention Site pilot bill, sits on the committee that wrote the medical regulations for the harm reduction centers, and helped end a statewide naloxone shortage in 2021. She is now advocating for better wages and support for the peer recovery workforce.

Plays and film

Playwright

Four Legs to Stand On toured schools and campuses across New England, followed by talkbacks with the audience. Resurfacing, co-written with Annie Brewster and Jonathan Adler, was performed around Boston in 2018 and 2019. In 2018, Trinity Repertory Company commissioned her to workshop a new play about the drug war, PTSD, mass incarceration and recovery. In 2023, she took the pilot of a TV short series on the history of harm reduction to New York Stage and Film. She was also a commissioned artist with A Pageant for Providence.

Clinical work

Drama therapist, 2014 onward

Ana Bess has worked with people in grief, trauma and addiction in homeless shelters, a veterans’ hospital, community health centers and San Quentin prison. She has run drama therapy groups with migrant and refugee teens in Rhode Island, and an art therapy camp for refugee teens in Eastern Ukraine.

About

Ana Bess Moyer Bell is a drama therapist, playwright, researcher and cultural activist working at the intersection of storytelling, public health and advocacy. A Rhode Island native and former Providence resident, she specializes in translating stories and lived experience into creative interventions made with communities, rather than simply for them.

Her work in Providence spans immigrant communities, recovery and harm reduction, and the arts, with collaborations including RIDOH, EOHHS, BHDDH and the Rhode Island U.S. Attorney’s Office. She served on Governor McKee’s transition team, took part in Leadership Rhode Island, and was a commissioned artist with Trinity Repertory Company and A Pageant for Providence.

Her life’s mission is to ensure dignity and unconditional support for people who use drugs, and to help find alternatives to the war on drugs. She holds an M.A. in Drama Therapy from New York University (summa cum laude) and is a Registered Drama Therapist with the North American Drama Therapy Association.
